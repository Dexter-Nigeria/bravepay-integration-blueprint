02

The answer, up front

You asked for a clear answer to one question: if BankOne is the core banking engine and NIBSS provides the rails, what should BravePay own and what should it delegate?

The dividing line

BankOne owns money. BravePay owns everything that is not money.

Concretely: BankOne becomes the single source of truth for customers-as-bank-customers, accounts, NUBANs, balances, the general ledger, and every posting. BravePay becomes the experience and business-tooling layer — identity and session, product surfaces, invoicing, payroll, expenses, budgeting, notifications, admin workflow, and the analytical reporting the core cannot give you. BravePay records money movement for its own product features; it never decides money movement.

That line is not a preference, it is forced by three things: BankOne is the licensed core with the GL and the regulatory returns; NIBSS settlement happens at institution level, not app level; and BravePay's current ledger cannot survive examination — it is a mutable balance column with no double-entry journal behind it bravepay code.

What must be removed or redesigned

  1. BankAccount.balance as a writable field, and the entire local mutation surface with it — debit, credit, _apply, lock, lock_accounts, update_balance. These were correct for a self-hosted ledger and are actively dangerous once BankOne is authoritative, because they let two systems write the same number.
  2. Transaction reference generation. BravePay emits REF{8 hex}-{4 digits} — sixteen characters bravepay code. BankOne caps transaction references at twelve documented. Every reference must be regenerated to a 12-character scheme before a single transfer can be attempted.
  3. The empty reports app (zero models, a three-line views file) should be built against a read replica or warehouse, not against live transaction tables.
  4. The payments app has three models and no URLs — it is a ledger that was started and abandoned. Retire it rather than extending it; its job moves to BankOne.

Response contract

Every money-movement call returns the same shape, and the interpretation rules are precise documented:

{
  "IsSuccessful": true,          // did the REQUEST succeed (not the transaction)
  "ResponseCode": "00",          // did the TRANSACTION succeed
  "ResponseMessage": "Approved by Financial Institution",
  "Reference": "A1908096119"
}
  • IsSuccessful: false — system error or invalid request. Retry with the same reference.
  • IsSuccessful: true + ResponseCode: "00" — transaction succeeded.
  • ResponseCode in the pending set (91, 06, x06) — indeterminate. Run a TSQ after the interval for that transaction type.
  • No response at all — indeterminate. Run a TSQ.
  • Any other code — the transaction failed. Retry only with a new reference.

Interbank transfer statuses are Pending, Failed, Successful, Reversed, and SuccessfulButFeeNotTaken. That last one is a real state your model must carry: the customer's money moved, your fee did not.

04

Three facts that shape everything

Most of the non-obvious design in this document traces back to three properties of the BankOne API. They are worth stating before the architecture, because they explain why the architecture looks the way it does.

1 · There are no webhooks

documented ask qore Across all 78 documentation pages there is no webhook, no callback URL, no notification endpoint, no event feed. The words do not appear. Integration is strictly request/response, and the documented mechanism for resolving an unknown outcome is Transaction Status Query polling, with per-transaction-type intervals (60 seconds for local funds transfer, debit and credit).

The consequence you cannot design around

BravePay cannot be told when money arrives. An inbound NIP credit from another bank lands in BankOne, and nothing pushes that fact to you. To notify a customer that they have been paid, BravePay must poll — GetTransactions per account, or BankOneBalanceEnquiry for change detection.

At a few thousand accounts, polling every account every minute is roughly 1.4 million calls a day. That is the single most important thing to raise with Qore before you finalise the design: is there a credit-notification feed, a settlement file, or a bulk transaction export not covered by the public docs? Section 20 phrases the question. Budget and architecture change materially depending on the answer.

2 · The published live endpoints are plaintext HTTP, with the token in the URL

documented Of the production URLs printed in the documentation, 94 use http://. One uses https:// — the iRestrict login portal. On the Corebanking half, the credential travels as a query parameter:

http://api.mybankone.com/BankOneWebAPI/api/Customer/CreateCustomer/version?authToken={{your_authtoken}}

Two distinct problems. Plaintext transport exposes customer PII, BVNs and the credential itself to anyone on the path. And a credential in a query string is written to access logs, proxy logs, and any intermediary's request history by default — it leaks even over TLS.

Treat this as a blocking pre-production question

This does not meet the CBN Risk-Based Cybersecurity Framework's expectations for a licensed institution, and it sits badly against the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the January 2027 payment-data localisation directive. Before signing off staging, get Qore to confirm in writing that production is reachable over TLS and whether header-based authentication is available on the Corebanking API. If the answer is "HTTPS works, the docs are stale", that is fine — but it needs to be in writing, and your client must pin to https:// and refuse to downgrade.

Interim control if TLS is confirmed but header auth is not: terminate all BankOne traffic through a single egress proxy that strips authToken from its own logs, and scope the token by IP allowlist (BankOne supports this — see below).

3 · Transaction references are capped at 12 characters

documented Both TransactionReference (interbank) and RetrievalReference (local transfer, debit, credit, TSQ, reversal) are documented as "maximum of 12 characters". BravePay currently generates:

# payments/models.py:72, transactions/models.py:122
return f"REF{uuid.uuid4().hex[:8]}-{last_4_timestamp}"   # -> "REF3f9a2b1c-4821"  = 16 chars

bravepay code Sixteen characters, four over. And MoneyMovement.reference_id is TRX_{user.id}_{8 hex}, which is variable-length and also overruns.

This is trivially fixable but it is load-bearing, because the reference is your only idempotency key. There is no Idempotency-Key header anywhere in the API. The rule set is:

  • Reuse the same reference when retrying an indeterminate call — that is what makes the retry safe.
  • Issue a new reference only after a definite failure.
  • Never reuse a reference across two different customer intents.

A 12-character scheme with adequate entropy and no coordination cost:

# 12 chars: 4-char epoch-second in base36 (~ 47 days of range, wraps harmlessly)
# + 8 chars of base36 randomness (36^8 ~ 2.8e12)
import secrets, time
ALPHABET = "0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ"

def b36(n, width):
    s = ""
    while n:
        n, r = divmod(n, 36)
        s = ALPHABET[r] + s
    return s.rjust(width, "0")[-width:]

def bankone_reference() -> str:
    return b36(int(time.time()), 4) + b36(secrets.randbits(48), 8)   # e.g. "R4K29FQ7B3XM"

Store it on the BravePay transaction as a dedicated bankone_reference column with a unique constraint, separate from your existing human-facing reference. Do not overload one field for both jobs — the customer-facing reference should stay readable, and the BankOne reference must stay exactly 12 characters forever.

05

BravePay ↔ BankOne architecture

How BravePay talks to BankOne

Not from the Django request/response cycle. Every BankOne call is either (a) a read behind a cache, or (b) a write executed by a background worker under a durable intent record. The reason is section 04's first fact: outcomes are frequently indeterminate and resolve by polling, which a synchronous HTTP handler cannot wait for.

The one rule that prevents most production incidents

An intent record is created and committed before the BankOne call is made, never after. If BravePay crashes between the call and the response, the intent is still there, still carrying its 12-character reference, and the TSQ sweeper will resolve it. A design that writes the record after a successful response loses money silently.

Authentication and authorization

Three separate trust boundaries. Do not conflate them.

  1. Customer → BravePay. Your existing JWT plus PIN/biometric step-up. Unchanged by this migration. Step-up must be required for any operation that moves money, not just at login.
  2. BravePay → BankOne. A single long-lived authToken created in the iRestrict portal, scoped by service group and locked to an IP allowlist documented. This is an institution credential, not a per-user one — BankOne has no idea which of your customers initiated a call. Everything you know about who did what has to come from your own audit log.
  3. Staff → BravePay admin. Your existing role_permission app. Maker-checker on high-value operations lives here, because BankOne will not enforce it for you on an API call.

Token strategy

Documentation says to set token expiry to "a minimum of 1 year" documented. Issue separate tokens per service group — one for read-only account and customer queries, one for transfers, one for card operations — so a leak of the reporting token cannot move money. Map each to the specific egress IP of the gateway. Store them in your platform's secret manager, never in .env committed anywhere, and record the expiry in your calendar: an unnoticed token expiry is a total outage.

Idempotency, retries and error handling

There is no idempotency header. The reference is the idempotency key, and the retry rules differ by outcome — this is the single most error-prone part of the integration, so encode it once, centrally, and never let a caller improvise.

Never auto-reverse an interbank transfer

The documentation is unambiguous: "A reversal request should NEVER be initiated for INTERBANK transfer irrespective of the transfer or TSQ status" documented. Reversals are permitted only for DEBIT, CREDIT and LOCALFUNDSTRANSFER, and only after the transaction has been successful for 90 seconds. Enforce this in code, not in a runbook — a well-meaning retry loop that reverses a NIP transfer creates a loss you cannot claw back.

06

BankOne ↔ NIBSS

The important structural point: BravePay will not hold a NIBSS connection, and in all likelihood neither will your MFB directly. BankOne aggregates.

NIP participation

ask nibss NIP admits banks, microfinance banks and mobile money operators. In practice the register splits into a small number of direct participants (the commercial banks) and several hundred indirect ones reached through aggregation — the majority of which are MFIs. Institutions without a CBN account settle through a settlement bank, against collateral deposited with that bank.

What you need to establish with your own institution, because it determines your liquidity model and not just your integration:

  • Which settlement bank holds the position, and what collateral is posted.
  • Whether your MFB appears in the NIP register under its own sort code, or under BankOne's.
  • The settlement windows, and therefore how long your float is exposed.
  • Whether inbound NIP credits to your NUBANs are visible to you in near-real time or only at settlement.

Name-enquiry session IDs behave differently in production

ask nibss Practitioner reports describe NIP session-ID validation on funds transfer as compulsory in UAT but expected to be disabled in production, on the grounds that leaving it on causes transfer failures to most banks. Your code should therefore always obtain and send a session ID, but must not treat a missing or rejected session ID as a hard client-side block in production. Confirm the exact behaviour with Qore during certification rather than discovering it on live traffic.

07

Transaction lifecycles

Account opening + KYC + NUBAN

One BankOne call does customer and account together, and it carries its own idempotency key.

  1. Customer completes onboarding in the Flutter app. BravePay holds the local identity.
  2. BravePay verifies NIN via QoreID and BVN via Account/BVN/GetBVNDetails. Compare the returned name and DOB against what the customer entered — a mismatch is a KYC stop, not a warning.
  3. BravePay calls CreateCustomerAndAccount with TransactionTrackingRef, ProductCode, BVN, NationalIdentityNo, PhoneNo, Email, AccountTier.
  4. Response returns AccountNumber, CustomerID, FullName.
  5. BravePay stores both identifiers and stops generating account numbers itself.

Account creation is genuinely idempotent

TransactionTrackingRef is documented as "a way to track that an account has already been created", and a replay returns "CreationMessage": "Account has already been created." with the original account number documented. So the safe pattern is: persist your tracking ref first, then call, and retry the same ref freely on any failure. You cannot double-open an account this way.

Retire BravePay's account-number generator on day one of this phase

Today BankAccount.generate_account_number() returns the last ten digits of the customer's phone number, and account_number_hash is a SHA-256 of the same bravepay code. That is not a NUBAN, it is not check-digit valid, and NIBSS will not route to it. It must be replaced by the BankOne-issued number, and the phone-derived value must be migrated out rather than left as a fallback.

Transfer to another Nigerian bank — the full path

Transfer between two BravePay accounts

Simpler, and it stays inside BankOne — CoreTransactions/LocalFundsTransfer with FromAccountNumber, ToAccountNumber, amount in kobo, a 12-character RetrievalReference, and an optional Fee. Same commit-then-call discipline; TSQ interval is 60 seconds; reversal is permitted here after 90 seconds if you need it.

Note what disappears: BravePay's current complete_payment(), which debits one local row and credits another, has no role. Both legs happen inside BankOne's GL as a single core operation.

Funding an account

Two distinct paths, and they behave differently:

  • Inbound NIP from another bank. Lands directly in the customer's NUBAN. BravePay learns about it only by polling. This is the flow most affected by the missing webhook.
  • Card or gateway top-up. Money reaches your collection account at the gateway, then must be moved into the customer's account. That second leg is CoreTransactions/Credit, which credits a customer account and debits a GL you nominate via GLCode. Your collection GL and the gateway's settlement report are what you reconcile against.

Receiving money — the polling design

inferred Until Qore confirms a notification feed, the workable pattern is tiered polling rather than uniform polling:

TierWhich accountsIntervalCall
HotSession active in the last 15 minutes, or expecting a known inbound30–60sBankOneBalanceEnquiry, then GetTransactions on change
WarmActive in the last 30 days10–15 minGetTransactions windowed since last cursor
ColdEverything elseNightlyGetTransactions for the full day, feeds reconciliation

Balance enquiry is the cheap change-detector; transaction fetch is the expensive truth. Poll the first, and only call the second when the balance has moved. Keep a per-account cursor so the nightly sweep is bounded, and make the nightly sweep authoritative — it is what catches anything the hot path missed.

08

Transaction state machine

BravePay's current model has three states — pending, success, failed bravepay code. That is not enough to represent an integration where the most common failure mode is not knowing. Nine states, and every transition is either an API outcome or a timer.

StateCustomer seesFundsExit
INITIATEDProcessingHeld, not movedCall attempted → SUBMITTED
SUBMITTEDSendingHeldResponse classified
INDETERMINATESendingHeld — never releasedTSQ, or escalation after 5 tries
SETTLEDSentMovedTerminal (reversal only for local types)
FAILEDFailed, funds returnedReleasedTerminal. New reference for any retry.
REVERSINGRefund in progressMoving backReversal response
REVERSEDRefundedReturnedTerminal
MANUAL_REVIEWUnder review — we will update youHeldOps decision, logged with an actor
ABANDONEDNothing happenedReleasedIntent committed, call never made

Two rules that make this safe

Never release held funds on an indeterminate outcome. The temptation is to unblock the customer; the result is a double spend when the TSQ later reports success.

Never move backwards. Model transitions as an explicit allowed-set and reject anything else at the database layer. A late TSQ response arriving after a manual resolution must not be able to overwrite it.

On utility and SageCloud

This is a genuine trade-off, not an obvious win. Staying on SageCloud keeps a working integration and probably better biller coverage, but every airtime purchase then debits through BankOne and fulfils through a third party — two systems to reconcile per transaction, and a failure window where the customer is debited and not credited. Moving to BankOne's bills payment makes debit and fulfilment one operation with one reference and one TSQ. Recommendation: compare BankOne's biller list against SageCloud's before deciding. If coverage is comparable, consolidate; if not, keep SageCloud and accept the dual reconciliation, but move the debit to CoreTransactions/Debit against a dedicated GL so at least the money side is single-sourced.

Data architecture & ownership

The shadow ledger

BravePay needs its own copy of postings — not to be authoritative, but because you cannot build spend analytics, category charts, invoice matching or a fast transaction list on top of a paginated third-party API. The discipline that keeps this safe is that the shadow ledger is append-only and derived: it is never written by a customer action, only by the ingestion of a confirmed BankOne posting.

# transactions/models.py — shape, not final migration

class TransferIntent(models.Model):
    """What the customer asked for. Written before any BankOne call."""
    id                 = UUIDField(primary_key=True)
    account            = FK("accounts.BankAccount")
    kind               = CharField(choices=["LOCAL", "INTERBANK", "BILL", "CREDIT", "DEBIT"])
    amount_kobo        = BigIntegerField()          # integer kobo, never Decimal naira
    currency           = CharField(default="NGN")
    bankone_reference  = CharField(max_length=12, unique=True)   # the idempotency key
    state              = CharField(choices=NINE_STATES, default="INITIATED")
    counterparty       = JSONField()                # account, bank code, resolved name
    nip_session_id     = CharField(null=True)
    tsq_attempts       = PositiveSmallIntegerField(default=0)
    next_tsq_at        = DateTimeField(null=True, db_index=True)
    last_response      = JSONField(null=True)       # verbatim, token stripped
    created_at         = DateTimeField(auto_now_add=True)

    class Meta:
        constraints = [CheckConstraint(check=Q(amount_kobo__gt=0), name="amount_positive")]
        indexes = [Index(fields=["state", "next_tsq_at"])]      # drives the sweeper


class LedgerEntry(models.Model):
    """A posting mirrored from BankOne. Append-only. Never written by a request."""
    account            = FK("accounts.BankAccount")
    bankone_txn_id     = CharField(unique=True)     # dedupe key for ingestion
    intent             = FK(TransferIntent, null=True)   # null for inbound credits
    direction          = CharField(choices=["DR", "CR"])
    amount_kobo        = BigIntegerField()
    balance_after_kobo = BigIntegerField(null=True)
    value_date         = DateField()
    narration          = TextField()
    ingested_at        = DateTimeField(auto_now_add=True)

Store money as integer kobo, everywhere

BankOne takes and returns amounts in kobo as strings documented. BravePay currently stores naira as DecimalField(max_digits=15, decimal_places=2) bravepay code. Converting at every boundary is where rounding bugs are born. Store BigIntegerField kobo internally, convert once at the presentation edge, and you eliminate an entire class of defect.

The sweeper is the backbone

A single Celery beat task, every 30 seconds: select intents where state = INDETERMINATE and next_tsq_at <= now(), ordered by age, bounded batch size. Run the TSQ, apply the transition, set the next due time with backoff, increment the attempt count, escalate at five. That one loop resolves most of the table above.

Ledger & reconciliation

Who keeps which book

BankOne keeps the double-entry general ledger, the chart of accounts, and the postings that a regulator will examine. BravePay keeps the shadow ledger — a derived, append-only mirror — and the intent log, which BankOne does not have and cannot reconstruct. Reconciliation is the process of proving those two agree, plus proving that every intent reached a terminal state.

Three daily reconciliations

  1. Intent completeness. No intent older than 24 hours may sit in a non-terminal state. This catches lost work, and it is the one most teams forget because it compares BravePay to itself.
  2. Posting agreement. For every account with activity: count and sum of shadow-ledger entries against GetTransactions for the same window. Mismatches produce exceptions, never corrections.
  3. Balance agreement. Cached balance against BankOneBalanceEnquiry. A divergence means the shadow ledger has drifted, and the ledger — not the balance — is what needs repair.

Exception management

Every mismatch becomes a durable record with a type, both sides of the discrepancy, an assignee, and an audit trail of what was done. Categories worth separating: timing (posted after the window closed — resolves itself), missing in BravePay (ingestion gap — re-fetch), missing in BankOne (serious: BravePay believes something happened that did not), and amount mismatch (most serious: stop and investigate before any further movement on that account).

Settlement is a separate book again

ask nibss Deferred net settlement means your NIP position settles at CBN in windows, not per transaction. So there is a third set of records — settlement reports from your settlement bank — that must agree with BankOne's interbank postings. Establish early who receives those files, in what format, and whether BankOne ingests them for you or hands them over. This is the reconciliation most likely to be discovered late.

Financial reporting

Regulatory returns — the CBN and NDIC monthly rendition, due within 14 days of month end — come from BankOne. Do not rebuild them. BravePay's reports app should serve management and product reporting only: cohort behaviour, product mix, channel performance, spend categories. Different audience, different source, different system.

Cutover sequence

Do not flip everything at once. Read paths first — show BankOne balances alongside local ones and compare for a week without acting. Then new accounts only, so a defect touches only new customers. Open questions

Take these to Qore before phase 2 exits. The first three can change the architecture; the rest change effort.

For Qore — architecture-changing

  1. Is there any mechanism to be notified of an inbound credit — webhook, callback, message queue, SFTP file, or bulk export — outside the published API? If not, what call volume is acceptable for polling, and what are the rate limits?
  2. Is the production API reachable over TLS? Documentation prints 94 production URLs as http://. Confirm HTTPS in writing, and state whether the Corebanking API accepts the auth token in a header rather than the query string.
  3. Where is our data hosted, and does that satisfy the CBN directive requiring payment transaction data generated in Nigeria to be stored and managed in Nigeria from 1 January 2027?

For Qore — effort-changing

  1. Rate limits per token and per endpoint; behaviour when exceeded.
  2. Published SLA and historical uptime for the channels API.
  3. Is mutual TLS or request signing available?
  4. Does a bulk or paginated transaction export exist beyond GetTransactions per account?
  5. Is NIBSS Direct Debit available, or are standing orders the only recurring-debit facility?
  6. Which GL codes are available to us for collection, fees and suspense, and who configures them?

  8. How is the sandbox seeded, and can we simulate pending, failed and reversed outcomes on demand?
